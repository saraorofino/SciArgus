#Machine Learning and AI
Research on the application of machine learning and artificial intelligence to biodiversity science, ecosystem monitoring, conservation planning, environmental management, and conservation decision-making. Relevant topics include foundation models, multimodal AI, computer vision, remote sensing, conservation genomics, and automated biodiversity monitoring. We are particularly interested in applications involving marine and coastal ecosystems, coral reefs, seagrass habitats, fisheries, plastic pollution, and environmental monitoring. We welcome both methodological advances and applied studies that improve understanding, measurement, prediction, or management of biodiversity systems. We also seek evaluations of AI capabilities and limitations in conservation, including benchmarks, governance, ethics, transparency, reliability, and the opportunities and risks associated with deploying AI in conservation practice.

#Causal Impact Estimation
Research evaluating the causal effects of conservation interventions, policies, programs, technologies, or management actions on biodiversity, ecological, social, or environmental outcomes. Relevant studies include methodological advances and applied impact evaluations using experimental and quasi-experimental approaches, including randomized control trials, natural experiments, matching, synthetic controls, difference-in-differences, and regression discontinuity. Particular areas of interest include protected areas, restoration interventions, fisheries management, bycatch mitigation technologies/practices, conservation incentive programs, marine conservation, plastic pollution policies, and other environmental interventions where causal effects can be credibly estimated. We are especially interested in spatial impact evaluation and causal machine learning. Studies focused solely on prediction, association, or trend analysis without a clear causal identification strategy are of lower relevance.

#Conservation Science
Research, reviews, syntheses, and perspectives that evaluate the effectiveness, direction, governance, and future of conservation science and practice. Relevant topics include evidence-based conservation, decision-science, conservation governance, implementation science, conservation strategy, institutional performance, funding models, policy effectiveness, scaling of conservation interventions. We are particularly interested in analyses that assess what conservation approaches work, under what conditions they succeed or fail, how conservation organizations and institutions can improve outcomes, and how conservation science should evolve in response to emerging environmental challenges. Relevant contributions include evaluations of conservation practice, strategic lessons from successes and failures, critiques of existing approaches, and forward-looking perspectives on the future of the field. Primary ecological studies without broader implications for conservation practice are of lower relevance.

#Conservation Incentives
Research on the design, implementation, governance, performance, and evaluation of incentive-based approaches to conservation. Relevant topics include payment for ecosystem services, biodiversity credits, offsets, mitigation banking, habitat banking, conservation agreements, conservation finance, outcome-based financing, voluntary and compliance markets, debt-for-nature swaps, and rights-based resource management. We are particularly interested in applications affecting marine ecosystems, fisheries, coastal habitats, species conservation, and biodiversity protection. Relevant contributions include empirical evaluations, economic analyses, governance studies, critiques of market design, assessments of social and ecological outcomes, and perspectives on the institutional conditions necessary for successful conservation incentive programs. Papers examining incentives for fisheries sustainability, bycatch reduction, habitat stewardship, restoration, or biodiversity outcomes are particularly relevant. Studies focused primarily on carbon markets or sustainable finance without a clear biodiversity or conservation connection are of lower relevance.

#Restoration Ecology
Research on the design, implementation, scaling, monitoring, and evaluation of ecological restoration and recovery efforts. Relevant topics include coral reef restoration, coral aquaculture and land-based coral farming, seagrass restoration, habitat restoration, species recovery, restoration planning, restoration effectiveness, restoration economics, restoration governance, and long-term restoration outcomes. We welcome studies comparing restoration approaches, identifying drivers of success and failure, evaluating ecological and social outcomes, and examining how restoration can be scaled to meet global biodiversity and climate goals. Relevant contributions include empirical studies, syntheses, methodological developments, monitoring frameworks, and perspectives on improving restoration practice. Particular interest is given to restoration in marine and coastal ecosystems and to studies that inform real-world conservation implementation.

#Climate Adaptation
Research on the use of genetics, genomics, and evolutionary principles to support biodiversity conservation and ecological resilience. Relevant topics include population genomics, conservation genetics, landscape genomics, adaptive variation, local adaptation, genetic diversity, connectivity, thermal tolerance, climate resilience, evolutionary responses to environmental change, and genetic approaches to restoration. We are particularly interested in research that improves understanding of adaptive capacity in changing environments, including assisted gene flow, assisted evolution, climate-informed restoration, resilience forecasting, and the genetic basis of responses to stressors such as warming, pollution, and habitat degradation. Relevant contributions include methodological advances, empirical studies, reviews, and applications that inform conservation management and restoration decision-making. Papers focusing on California, the United States, North America or the pacific are of particular relevance.

#Bycatch Mitigation
Research on reducing biodiversity impacts from capture fisheries while maintaining sustainable fisheries and livelihoods. Relevant topics include bycatch mitigation technologies, selective fishing gear, fisheries management, fishing mortality, threatened species interactions, harvest strategies, spatial management, dynamic ocean management, observer programs, electronic monitoring, and fisheries compliance. We are particularly interested in research relevant to sharks, rays, marine mammals, sea turtles, seabirds, and other vulnerable taxa affected by fishing activities. Relevant contributions include evaluations of management interventions, gear innovations, monitoring approaches, socioecological analyses, and studies examining tradeoffs between conservation objectives and fisheries performance. Studies from both industrial and small-scale fisheries are relevant.

#Plastic Pollution Science and Policy
Research on the sources, transport, fate, impacts, monitoring, management, and prevention of plastic pollution across terrestrial, freshwater, coastal, and marine environments. Relevant topics include plastic leakage, marine debris, litter monitoring, waste management systems, agricultural plastics, microplastics, plastic transport pathways, pollution source attribution, environmental exposure, human health implications, and socioeconomic impacts of plastic pollution. We are particularly interested in studies evaluating interventions to reduce plastic pollution, including product redesign, reuse systems, extended producer responsibility, packaging policies, and national or international regulatory approaches. Relevant contributions include monitoring methods, environmental assessments, policy evaluations, systems analyses, and studies linking plastic production and use to environmental and human outcomes.

#Environmental Monitoring
Research using remote sensing and related monitoring technologies to measure, map, assess, and track environmental conditions and ecological change. Relevant topics include satellite remote sensing, airborne sensing, drone-based monitoring, habitat mapping, change detection, environmental indicators, sensor development, image analysis, and large-scale ecosystem observation. We are particularly interested in applications involving coral reefs, seagrass habitats, fisheries, coastal ecosystems, biodiversity monitoring, restoration monitoring, plastic pollution detection, agricultural plastic mapping, and environmental risk assessment. Relevant contributions include methodological developments, operational monitoring systems, validation studies, and applications that improve environmental measurement, decision-making, and conservation outcomes.
